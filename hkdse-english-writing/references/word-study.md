# Word study and confused forms

## Roots, prefixes, and suffixes

1. Predict the word class from its position. Check whether the surrounding words indicate approval, opposition, quantity, direction, time, or intensity.
2. Identify a likely prefix, root, or suffix and propose a meaning. Examples include *a-/an-* (without), *anti-* (against), *ante-* (before), *ambi-* (both), *aqua* (water), *auto-* (self), *bi-* (two), *bene-* (good), *biblio-* (book), *bio-* (life), *circum-* (around), *-cide* (killing), *demo-* (people), *-cracy* (rule), *dia-* (through), *dis-* (apart or reversal), *-graph* (writing or recording), *inter-* (between), *micro-* (small), *multi-* (many), *post-* (after), *re-* (again or back), *trans-* (across), and *-ology* (study of).
3. Substitute the inferred sense in the sentence. Check against nearby definitions, comparisons, contrasts, examples, or consequences. Word parts give clues and can have multiple histories or senses.
4. For vocabulary practice, record the meaning in context, word class, a useful collocation, and a new sentence. Group related words by meaning or form and test recall in a plausible sentence.

## Confusable words

| Contrast | How to test it |
| --- | --- |
| affect / effect | The change *affected* attendance. Its *effect* was immediate. Check the action or result, and recognise less common uses such as *effect change*. |
| allusion / illusion | An *allusion to* a story is a reference. An *illusion* is a misleading appearance or impression. |
| advice / advise | Give *advice*, a normally uncountable noun. *Advise* someone to do something. |
| rise / raise / arise | Attendance *rose*. We *raised* the limit. A problem *arose*. Check whether a direct object is required. |
| lie / lay | *Lie* down (past *lay*). *Lay* the tools on the table (past *laid*). The verb meaning tell an untruth uses *lied*. |
| borrow / lend | The receiver *borrows from* someone. The giver *lends something to* someone. |
| say / tell / speak / talk | *Say* something to someone, *tell* someone something, *speak* a language, *talk to* someone about an issue. |
| job / work | A *job* can be counted. *Work* is generally uncountable when referring to labour. |
| accept / except | We *accept* applications every day *except* Sunday. |
| maybe / may be | *Maybe* modifies the statement. *May be* is a modal followed by a verb. |
| answer / reply / reward | *Answer* a question, *reply to* an email, offer a *reward for* information. |
| fewer / less | Use *fewer* with countable items and *less* with quantities or uncountable nouns, with conventional measure expressions such as *less than two hours*. |

The worksheets practise judging a sentence, identifying the word class, choosing a form in a gap, and explaining the correction. For a drill, vary homophones, near-synonyms, word families, verb patterns, prepositions, and countability. Ask for the reason and a new sentence after the learner makes a choice. ## Description and register banks

Distinguish a character's trait, temporary emotion, the text's tone, and the mood it creates. For descriptions of people, choose a few relevant details of hair, face, movement, or voice and explain what the viewpoint character notices. Use conventional adjective order where several adjectives are needed. Use a colour idiom such as *get the green light* only when its figurative meaning fits the sentence and audience.
