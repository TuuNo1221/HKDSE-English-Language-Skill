# Paper 4 speaking

For 2027, Part A is a discussion based on a short stimulus, with 10 minutes of preparation and 8 minutes of discussion for a group of four. Part B is an individual response on a related question, with one minute per candidate. Use the instructions for the set being practised. [HKEAA 2027 framework](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/HKDSE/Subject_Information/eng_lang/2027hkdse-e-elang.pdf)

## Part A group interaction

1. Identify the group's role, what it must discuss or decide, and each prompt. Jot down keywords for a possible view, a reason and example, and a question that would help the group compare options. Use the stimulus as an opening for discussion.
2. Take a turn when you can add something. Respond to a specific point, give a reason or concrete illustration, then offer a genuine question or leave room for others to respond. Stay involved as the topic develops.
3. Invite a quieter participant with a focused question. Ask for clarification when an idea is unclear. When views differ, acknowledge the previous speaker's reasoning and explain a practical trade-off. If the group stalls, offer one specific alternative or return to an uncovered prompt.
4. Near the end, sum up any real agreement and unresolved choices when useful. Use natural pace, audible speech, and pauses that let others take a turn.

**Original practice exchange.** If another candidate suggests a lunchtime repair workshop, a student might say, “That would make it easy for students to drop in. We have only half an hour, so could we limit the first session to simple repairs? What jobs do you think would fit?”

## Part B individual response

Answer the actual question in the first sentence. Give a reason and develop it with an example, effect, or relevant comparison. If the question asks for a preference, compare the options on a criterion you can explain. If it asks for a recommendation, tell the examiner what you would do and why. Practise aloud within a minute and adapt the length to your pace.

**Original prompt.** “Should schools make repair workshops compulsory?” One possible plan is to recommend a short demonstration for everyone, explain how it gives all students basic safety knowledge, then suggest voluntary practice sessions for students who want more time with the tools.

## Run a mock or give feedback

- For a Part A mock, provide or use the stimulus, role and discussion points. In chat, play one or more candidate roles with short, distinct contributions. Wait for the learner on each turn and build on what they actually say. If the environment lacks a live timer, offer the official timing as a practice target and let the learner self-time.
- For a Part B mock, ask one related question and wait. Give tailored feedback or ask a relevant follow-up if the learner requests further practice. A model answer can follow their attempt or be given on request.
- In both parts, assess relevance, development, vocabulary and language, and organisation. For Part A, also check listening and interaction through observable replies and turn-taking. If audio is available, comment on intelligibility, pace, stress and pausing. If video is available, comment on observable nonverbal delivery. Text alone supports feedback on phrasing and the interaction recorded in the transcript.
- Prioritise one improvement and give a retry prompt. Use a supplied rubric when asked for a score and label any estimate as practice feedback.
