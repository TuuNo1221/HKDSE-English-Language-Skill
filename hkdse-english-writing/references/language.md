# Language and revision

Edit in this order unless the user requests a narrower pass.

1. **Task and clarity.** Check whether the sentence says who does what and why it matters. Replace vague nouns or inflated wording with specific actions.
2. **Sentence boundaries and meaning.** Repair run-ons, comma splices, fragments that obscure meaning, unclear pronouns, and misplaced modifiers. Split or join clauses according to the intended relation.
3. **Recurring grammar.** Give the student a pattern they can reuse. Mark one or two representative errors before checking repetitions.
4. **Style and mechanics.** Check spelling consistency, missing punctuation, and register. Add variety where it helps the reader follow the point or emphasis.

| Issue | What to check | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agreement | Find the head of the subject, even when extra nouns intervene. | The list of suggested activities **is** on the noticeboard. |
| Nouns and articles | Check countability and whether the noun is introduced, identifiable, or general. | She gave me **some advice** about **a** competition. **The** competition starts next week. |
| Verb complements | Check whether the verb takes an object, preposition, infinitive, or gerund in this sense. | We **discussed the plan**, then **agreed to test** it. We **suggested testing** it on Friday. |
| Tense and conditionals | Match the time and likelihood to the idea. | If the pilot **works**, we **will extend** it. If we **had** more space, we **could** admit another group. |
| Parallelism | Keep items serving the same grammatical function in matching forms. | The club aims to **reduce** waste, **share** equipment, and **teach** repair skills. |
| Clause punctuation | Join independent clauses with a period, conjunction, or appropriate punctuation. | The room was full, **so** we moved the session to the hall. |
| Cohesion | Choose a connector for the relationship it actually expresses. | The service is free. **However**, students still need to book a place. |
| Word choice | Check collocation, word class, and meaning in context. | The delay **affected** attendance. Its **effect** was strongest in the first session. |

Use time markers, pronouns with clear antecedents, and repeated key terms when they improve continuity. Keep formal requests courteous and direct, informal replies friendly, and speech sentences easy to say aloud. Use complex constructions when they express a genuine relation such as cause, condition, or concession.

For practice, select an error from the student's draft, explain the rule briefly, then give a targeted item that uses a different topic. Ask for a correction or a fresh sentence. If the user asks for a worksheet, put the answer key after the exercises.

## Sentence-pattern drills

Choose a pattern by meaning. Use *although* to concede a point, *so that* to show purpose, or *so ... that* to show a result. Keep the actor clear in reduced clauses, and preserve tense, agency, emphasis and certainty when rewriting.

- **Concession.** Combine “The hall is small. It can hold a lunchtime workshop.” Sample answer. “Although the hall is small, it can hold a lunchtime workshop.”
- **Purpose.** Combine “We posted a room map. First-time visitors would be able to find us.” Sample answer. “We posted a room map so that first-time visitors could find us.”
- **Advice.** Fix “I suggest you to book a place early.” Sample answer. “I suggest booking a place early,” or “I suggest that you book a place early.” Use “I advise you to book a place early” when practising the object-plus-infinitive pattern.
