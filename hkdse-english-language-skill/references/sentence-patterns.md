## Transformation checks

- Preserve speaker, actors, time, negation, degree of certainty, and the connection between ideas. Check that a concession, cause, purpose, result, or condition has kept its meaning.
- For a reduced participle clause, the main subject normally acts as the understood subject. In an absolute construction, the opening phrase supplies its own subject.
- Use commas around a non-defining clause. A defining clause identifies the referent. With embedded questions, keep statement word order.
- Keep the same kind of grammatical unit on both sides of a paired construction. Invert an auxiliary after a fronted negative or restrictive expression where the construction requires it. Use *than* with *no sooner* and *when* with *hardly* in these time patterns.
- Use *in case* for a precaution and *unless* for an exception. *But for* takes a noun phrase. *Supposing* introduces a possible condition. *As long as* and *provided that* can introduce requirements.
- Match advice to tone and complement. Use *should/ought to*, *had better (not) + base verb*, *advise/urge someone to*, *warn someone not to* or *against -ing*, *suggest -ing* or *suggest that someone (should) do something*, *recommend -ing* or a that-clause, *why not + base verb*, *how about -ing*, *let’s + base verb*, *be worth -ing*, *would rather/sooner + base verb*, *prefer X to Y*, and *it is high time + subject + past form*.
- If the learner gives a worksheet item, check its precise target and instructions first. Compare with the source key only after an attempt unless the learner requests the answer. If the key and a valid alternative differ, explain the grammatical and semantic distinction.

**Original drill.** Combine “The volunteers had just finished setting up. The first visitors arrived” with A32. Answer. “No sooner had the volunteers finished setting up than the first visitors arrived.” A natural alternative starts “Hardly had …” and uses “when”.
